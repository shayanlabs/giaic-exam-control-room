# 44. Full timed mock exam

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

The real exam is timed and high-stakes.

The best preparation is to sit a full mock under the same conditions: 100 multiple-choice questions in 140 minutes, phone in another room, no interruptions.

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### 100 MCQs in 140 minutes
That is 1.4 minutes per question on average. You must practice both knowledge and pacing.

### Phone in another room
Removes the temptation to look things up and trains the real exam conditions.

### Target a score of 90+, not just a pass at 70
Passing is the minimum. Aiming for 90+ forces you to close the remaining gaps and builds confidence.

### Mark every question you had to guess on
After the mock, the guesses are pure gold. They tell you exactly which topics still need work.

### The two different kinds of failure a mock reveals
There are two very different reasons to get a question wrong, and a timed mock is the only way to tell them apart. One is a knowledge gap — you simply didn't know the concept. The other is a pacing failure — you knew it, but ran out of time or rushed and picked wrong under pressure. Fixing a knowledge gap means going back to that topic's note. Fixing a pacing failure means practicing under the clock again, not re-studying content you already know. Mixing these two up wastes your remaining study time.

## Why would this be on the exam?
The exam itself is a timed performance. Practicing under the same constraints is the only way to discover pacing and knowledge problems before the real day.
