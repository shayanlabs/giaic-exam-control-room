# 8. Choosing Your Vertical

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

A vertical is a special version of the whole system for one profession — for example accounting, or healthcare, or law in one country.

Before you spend months building one, you run a careful check. The most important rule is simple: **if you do not have a real expert who will stay and own the knowledge, do not start**. Without that expert the whole thing is built on sand.

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### The screen
A structured filter that forces you to answer hard questions early: Is the knowledge available? Can we get the legal rights? Is there real demand? Do we have the right partners?

### The eight tests
Eight practical checks that surface the real risks before you invest heavy time or money.

### The launch gates
Clear “go / no-go” points. They stop the common mistake of starting to build and only later realizing a critical piece is missing.

### No committed domain expert means no vertical
This is the non-negotiable rule. An Outcome Architect or FDE without a committed expert will produce demos that look good and then fail in real use, because the knowledge is incomplete or wrong.

### The one test that can end everything by itself
Among the eight tests, one is special: Test 3 (Expert availability). If you fail this one test alone, the whole evaluation stops immediately — no matter how well the other seven scored. Why does this single test get veto power? Because the domain expert is the source of both the corpus (their written material) and the twin (their persona and judgment). Without a real, committed expert willing to license their knowledge, there is nothing true for the AI workers to be trained on — the entire trio (corpus, expert twin, builder) collapses. Everything else in the eight tests is a risk to manage; this one is a hard floor.

## Why would this be on the exam?
Building and selling a vertical is one of the main ways graduates are expected to earn money. The exam checks that you understand the decision process and the expert requirement.
