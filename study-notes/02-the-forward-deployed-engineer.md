# 2. The Forward Deployed Engineer

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

A normal software engineer sits in a big office far away. They invent a tool and send the same tool to many companies.

A Forward Deployed Engineer (FDE) does the opposite. They pack a bag, go to the customer’s real office, sit next to the people who do the work, and build the tool *right there* using the customer’s real computers and real data.

It is like the difference between a doctor who only reads your chart from another city and a doctor who sits in the room with you, looks at you, and starts treatment on the spot. The FDE is the second doctor.

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### Palantir origin
A company called Palantir started this job in the early 2010s. They called the people “Deltas” at first. Palantir’s customers were governments and big old companies. Those customers needed someone who could walk into a messy organization and make things work fast. Sometimes Palantir had more FDEs than normal software engineers.

### Vendor lock-in
Most companies that hire FDEs only teach them to use *their own* tools. So the FDE wires the customer tightly to one vendor. Later, if the customer wants to change tools, it is very hard and expensive — like a plumber who only knows how to install one brand of pipes. The book trains the version that is **not** locked to any vendor.

### The pod of one
In the old days, a company would send a team of 5 or 6 people to a customer for weeks. With AI workers, one trained human can command a team of Digital FTEs and do the same work (or more) alone. That is the “pod of one.”

### The third door (freelance)
You do not have to join a big company. The same skills also let you work for yourself and sell the service directly to customers.

### Important numbers and what they mean
- **729% rise in job postings**: In one year the number of FDE job ads grew almost 8 times. That means companies are desperate for these people.
- **About $190,000 median salary**: This is strong mid-to-senior engineer pay. Top people at big AI labs earn much more.
- **MIT’s 95% pilot failure number**: About 95 out of 100 custom AI projects inside companies show no clear money benefit. The AI is usually smart enough. The hard part is fitting it into the company’s messy real systems. FDEs exist to solve that fitting problem.
- **AWS $1 billion and Microsoft $2.5 billion**: In the same week in 2026, the two biggest cloud companies put a total of $3.5 billion into their own FDE teams. That shows how serious the market is.

### A worked example
Picture a mid-size accounting firm. A vendor-locked FDE would show up with one company's proprietary AI platform and wire the firm to it forever. A vendor-neutral FDE (the version this book trains) instead builds on open, portable patterns — skills, specs, MCP connectors — so the firm's Digital FTEs still work even if the underlying model or tool changes later. That portability is exactly why "vendor-neutral" is the qualifier the book keeps repeating: it's the difference between building the customer a captured asset and building them a real one.

## Why would this be on the exam?
The FDE is the market's name for the full skill set the book teaches when you take it inside a customer's company. The exam wants you to know where the job came from, why it pays well, and why vendor-neutral skills matter.
