# 6. System of Record

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

A normal search box is like asking a friend who sometimes remembers and sometimes guesses.

A **System of Record** is like the official rule book that the whole school agrees is the truth. It has a name on the cover (the owner), page numbers that never change, a date when it was last checked, and rules about who can write in it.

When an AI answers from the System of Record, it can point to the exact page. That is how you know the answer is not a guess.

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### Governance, not just retrieval
Retrieval = “I found something that says this.”
Governance = “This is the official, checked, versioned truth that the organization stands behind.”

### Named owner
Someone’s name is on it. If the content is wrong, that person is responsible.

### Versioning
You can see what the truth was last month and what it is today. Changes are recorded.

### Review
New content does not become official until someone has checked it.

### Access control
Not everyone (and not every AI) can read or change everything.

### Stable IDs
Every important piece has a permanent number or code so links and citations never break.

### Citations
When the AI answers, it can say “see section 4.2.” That is how trust is kept.

In the big model this is Layer 1. The same tool that holds the book will later hold accounting rules or medical protocols.

### A stable ID, explained with a real analogy
Think of a stable ID like a page reference in a legal textbook that never changes edition to edition — you can cite "section 4.2" today, and even after the book is updated next year, that citation still points to the right place (or clearly shows it moved). Without stable IDs, every update would silently break every citation an AI worker ever made, and nobody would notice until something went wrong.

## Why would this be on the exam?
Without a System of Record the AI is just a clever guesser. With it the AI becomes trustworthy. This idea sits under almost every serious agent system.
