# 43. Build the question bank

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

The course pages already contain many ready-made questions in the form of “Check Yourself,” “Test Your Understanding,” and Flashcards.

Your job is to pull all of them into one master bank so you can practice systematically instead of hoping you remember everything.

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### Compile every “Check Yourself,” “Test Your Understanding,” and Flashcards question
Do not rely on memory or scattered notes. Physically (or digitally) gather them into one place.

### Roughly 70+ ready-made questions already in exam format
You do not have to invent everything from scratch. A large fraction of the useful practice material is already written for you in exam-style form.

### Why this beats re-reading
Re-reading a chapter feels productive but is a weak way to learn — your brain recognizes the words and mistakes that recognition for real understanding. Answering a question forces actual retrieval: your brain has to reconstruct the answer from scratch, which is exactly the skill the real exam tests. This is called "active recall," and it's one of the most well-supported study techniques there is — a stack of ready-made questions is what makes it easy to actually do this instead of just re-reading notes.

## Why would this be on the exam?
Active recall is one of the highest-leverage study activities. Building the bank forces you to confront the actual questions the course considers important.
