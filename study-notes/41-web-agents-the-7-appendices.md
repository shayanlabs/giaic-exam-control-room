# 41. The 7 appendices

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

The web-agents course ends with seven short appendices that map each of the later major courses onto the web surface.

The single key idea is: **on the web you rent a harness; in the later courses you build one**.

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### Each later course mapped onto the web surface
The appendices show how concepts from loop engineering, harness engineering, evals, runtime, etc. appear when the agent lives primarily in a browser or remote-session environment.

### On the web you rent a harness; in the later courses you build one
This is the central contrast. Web products give you convenience and a pre-built (but limited) harness. The deeper courses teach you to construct the harness yourself so you own the control surface.

### The five verbs as a lens for what you're renting
Harness Engineering (a later course) organizes everything a harness does into five verbs: constrain (limit what the agent may do), inform (give it what it needs), verify (prove the work before it counts), correct (recover, then fix the system), and escalate (send it to a person). On the web you only get a real slice of two: you *inform* through a project's saved instructions and packaged skills, and you sit at the *escalate* gate through your phone. The other three — constrain, verify, correct — are done for you by the vendor, in ways you can't see or change. Knowing this table tells you exactly what you're trusting the vendor to do versus what you're actually deciding yourself.

### The harness war is industry-wide, not two companies
By 2026 the fight described in this course isn't just Claude Cowork versus ChatGPT Work — nearly every major lab has accepted that competition moved off the raw model and onto the harness around it. Google's Gemini Spark is a 24/7 cloud agent across Gmail, Docs, and Workspace; Microsoft's Copilot Tasks unifies Auto, Researcher, and Analyst modes inside Microsoft 365; Meta AI fights on distribution by living inside WhatsApp, Messenger, and Instagram. One layer down sits the managed-runtime front (Google's Gemini Enterprise Agent Platform, Microsoft's Agent 365) where the hard problem is governance — identity, policy, audit — not raw capability. And at the far end sits the open-engine front (DeepSeek V4, Qwen 3.6/3.7, Llama), where self-hosting the open weights is the only path that lets you own the entire harness, model included, which matters most when regulated or sensitive data rules out a vendor's custody. Same six-part shape, three different fronts of the same war.

## Why would this be on the exam?
It is the explicit bridge between the accessible web surface and the deeper engineering that follows. The exam expects you to understand the “rent vs build” distinction.
