# 11. FDE AF Model III — the business layer

## Simple Explanation (like you're 5)

A pretty diagram does not pay the bills. This part answers three money questions:
- Where does each layer make money?
- What does a profession need before it can become a real vertical?
- What does a successful project with a customer look like?

## Key Concepts Unpacked

### Where each layer earns
- Bottom layers mostly earn through the platform and licensing.
- Layer 2 earns through education and tools (almost no extra AI cost).
- Layer 3 earns through domain partnerships and vertical licensing.
- Layer 4 earns through real customer projects and ongoing operation of the Digital FTEs.

### The domain trio
For a vertical to be real it needs three things:
- **Corpus** — the body of knowledge (the rules, standards, protocols).
- **Map** — how that knowledge is organized so both humans and AIs can find things.
- **Reflexes** — the automatic skills and behaviors that act on the knowledge.

### The contract of success
Every serious customer project needs three clear numbers:
- **Baseline** — where the customer is today (measured).
- **Target** — where they need to be.
- **Acceptance criteria** — the exact definition of “done and good enough.”

### KPIs, adoption, evals
Success is not “the AI seemed smart.” Success is measured by business numbers, by whether real people actually use the system, and by hard tests that prove the workers keep doing good work.

### A worked example (the accounting firm)
Picture a graduate's first real engagement: a mid-size accounting firm. The baseline is measured honestly first — preparing one working-paper file currently takes 4 hours. The target, agreed in writing before any building starts, is 40 minutes. The acceptance criteria says the AI worker's output must be correct on the first pass 95% of the time, and the firm's own human reviewers must sign off. That's the contract of success in action: nobody starts building until both baseline and target are on paper. The finish line isn't a good demo — it's proof in production: real KPI numbers, real staff actually using the tool (adoption), and passing evals that show the worker keeps behaving correctly over time. All three matter, because an AI worker can pass every technical eval and still fail to move the business number if nobody adopts it.

## Why would this be on the exam?
The model is only useful if it can turn into real outcomes and real money. The exam checks that you understand the business side, not just the technical layers.
